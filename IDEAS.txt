1. Mint/redeem arbitrage (closest to the arb engine)

Many tokens have a second price besides the DEX price: a contract that mints or redeems them at a fixed or NAV rate. When the pool drifts from that rate, you can borrow, mint (or redeem), sell into the pool, and repay, all in one transaction.

Stablecoin PSMs: Sky/Spark PSM (USDC↔USDS/sUSDS at par), GHO’s GSM, crvUSD PegKeepers.
Liquity v2 (BOLD) and its forks: redemptions are atomic. When BOLD trades below $1, you buy it on a DEX, redeem for $1 of collateral, and sell the collateral. This is the classic flash-loan peg arb.
ERC-4626 vaults and LSTs with instant deposit: stS on Sonic, sAVAX, wstETH and similar. Only one direction is usually atomic. Instant deposit lets you profit when the DEX price is above NAV (mint and sell). Below NAV you’d hit an unstake queue.

This plugs straight into your graph: model a mint or redeem path as a synthetic edge with a fixed rate and effectively infinite depth (or capped by a mint limit). The triangle finder then discovers cycles like USDC → PSM → USDS → pool → USDC without new search logic. You’d add a new hop kind in HopEngine, a per-edge rate read, and handling for fees, mint caps, and pause flags.

Flash mints (ERC-3156, e.g. DAI). The asset is minted from nothing, then burned. Unlike a pool-backed loan, depth isn’t capped by any pool.
Mint/redeem arb on stablecoins and liquid restaking tokens, plus Pendle PT/YT mispricings. The limit is wherever redemption isn’t atomic, such as withdrawal queues.
Cross-venue in one transaction. Your notes on HyperEVM are a live example: HyperCore precompiles let one transaction arb an AMM against the order book.

2. Intent and Dutch-auction filling

UniswapX, 1inch Fusion, and CoW publish orders through off-chain APIs, so not having a mempool doesn’t matter. A Dutch order’s price decays over time. Once it crosses what you can get by routing through DEX pools, you fill the order and source the output inside the fill callback, with no inventory. UniswapX runs on Base and Arbitrum. Your route builder and pool-state reader are most of the work. The new parts are order polling and signature/reactor plumbing. Expect competition from professional fillers, but long-tail token pairs are thinner.

7. Contract ideas with fee revenue
A. Keeper jobs. Many protocols pay bounties for calling harvest(), rebase(), oracle updates, or auction settlements. These are boring, steady, and low-competition on small chains.
B. LP range manager vault. It rebalances concentrated-liquidity positions for a performance fee. This is real demand, but you’d be custodying other people’s funds, which means audits and liability.
